Vertical columns · deformation, buckling, and what Euler leaves out

A meeting-ready explanation that starts with axial shortening, builds the column buckling model, then extends it to eccentric loads, imperfections, bracing, and the limits of elastic Euler theory. Six practice questions ramp from fundamentals to design judgment.

压杆 · compression member稳定 · stability屈曲 · buckling

A vertical column under a centered load first shortens. Buckling is a different issue: the straight shape may stop being a stable equilibrium, so a small sideways displacement grows instead of disappearing. Keep material failure (crushing/yielding) and instability (buckling) as separate checks.

ExplanationPracticeBeyond Euler

1 · What does a loaded column do?

Axial deformation

A short, stocky column usually stays nearly straight and compresses. In the linear elastic range, Δ = PL/(AE).

Lateral deformation

A slender column can bow sideways. Compression acting through that bow creates a bending moment that pushes the bow farther out.

Failure mode

A column can fail by material crushing/yielding, by elastic or inelastic buckling, or by both interacting. The first limit reached governs.

2 · Why the straight column loses stability

Imagine pushing a ruler from its ends. If it is perfectly straight, the load is centered. Now nudge it sideways by a small amount Δ. The compressive force is no longer aligned with the bent centerline, so it produces a secondary moment:

M(x) = P · v(x)

Below a critical load, bending stiffness EI can restore the straight shape. At the critical load, the restoring effect and the P–Δ destabilizing effect balance. Above it, the straight shape is unstable. This is why buckling is called a stability problem, not simply a strength problem.

3 · Euler load and effective length

For a slender, initially straight, prismatic, elastic column with ideal end supports and a perfectly centered load, the first buckling load is:

Pcr = π²EI/(KL)² = π²EI/Le²
Le = KL   (effective length)

E measures material stiffness, I is the second moment of area about the buckling axis, L is physical length, and K represents end restraint. The column buckles about its weak axis, so use the smaller I. Common ideal K values: pinned–pinned 1.0; fixed–free 2.0; fixed–fixed 0.5; fixed–pinned about 0.7.

Scaling intuition: double L → one quarter of Pcr; double E or I → twice Pcr. Increasing depth can be powerful because rectangular I = bh³/12 has a cubic dependence on the depth.

4 · The buckling shape is a mode

Euler buckling is an eigenvalue problem: only certain loads admit a nonzero bent shape satisfying the end conditions. The lowest eigenvalue is the first mode and normally controls. For a pinned–pinned column, the first-mode shape is a half sine wave:

v(x) = Δmax sin(πx/L),   Pcr,1 = π²EI/L²

Higher modes have extra half-waves and larger ideal critical loads (for pinned ends, mode n has Pcr,n = n²Pcr,1). Real columns usually buckle in the first mode because it takes the least load.

5 · Go beyond the perfect Euler column

Eccentric loading

If the load misses the centroid by e, it creates a first-order moment M = Pe even before the column bows. The stress has both direct compression and bending: σ = P/A ± Mc/I. A more slender member also amplifies this bending as it deflects.

Initial crookedness and residual stress

No real column is perfectly straight or perfectly uniform. A small initial bow means P–Δ bending exists from the start. In practice, capacity is lower and failure is gradual around the ideal Euler threshold rather than a dramatic jump at one exact value.

Short, intermediate, and slender columns

Euler assumes elastic behavior. A short column may yield or crush before Euler buckling; a slender one may buckle elastically; an intermediate column can yield locally while it bends. Design standards use column curves or an inelastic/tangent-modulus model in this transition region, rather than applying Euler blindly.

Bracing and system effects

A brace helps only in the direction it restrains. A mid-height lateral brace can halve the unbraced length for that buckling direction, ideally multiplying Euler load by four. The brace itself must be stiff and strong enough to provide restraint; a drawn brace is not automatically an effective brace.

Model limits: Euler's Pcr is an ideal elastic bifurcation load, not a general safe working load. Real design must also consider imperfections, eccentricity, material yielding, local section buckling, connection behavior, and code resistance factors.

P1

A straight column under load

warm-up

A steel column is 3 m long, area 600 mm², E = 200 GPa, and carries 120 kN concentrically. Find its elastic shortening.

Hint

What quantity measures change in length, rather than loss of stability?

Show worked solution

Use Δ = PL/(AE). In N and mm, E = 200,000 N/mm², P = 120,000 N, and L = 3,000 mm.

Δ = (120,000)(3,000)/[(600)(200,000)] = 3.00 mm

This calculation describes axial strain while the column remains straight; it is not a buckling calculation.

P2

Changing the restraints

medium

A pinned–pinned steel round bar has d = 30 mm, L = 2 m, E = 200 GPa. Find its ideal Euler load. What would the same member's ideal load be if fixed–free?

Hint

Which restraint changes the effective length?

Show worked solution

I = πd⁴/64 = π(30)⁴/64 = 39,761 mm⁴. Use E = 200,000 N/mm² and L = 2,000 mm.

Pcr,pinned = π²EI/L² = 19.62 kN
Fixed–free: K = 2, so Pcr = 19.62/2² = 4.90 kN

The cantilever column is four times less stable in this ideal comparison because its effective length doubles.

P3

A rectangular member and a brace

medium

A 120 × 60 mm rectangular column is pinned at both ends. Which direction is the weak buckling direction? By what factor does a sound mid-height brace in that direction raise its ideal Euler load?

Hint

Check both axes before choosing a direction.

Show worked solution

For a rectangle, I = bh³/12 with the dimension in the bending direction cubed. The weak-axis inertia is 120(60)³/12 = 2.16×10⁶ mm⁴; the strong-axis inertia is 60(120)³/12 = 8.64×10⁶ mm⁴. It buckles first in the 60 mm depth direction. A mid-height brace halves effective length for that direction, so Pcr,new/Pcr,old = (L/(L/2))² = 4.

P4

An off-centre force

medium-hard

A short steel post has A = 2,000 mm² and section modulus W = 40,000 mm³. A 60 kN compressive load acts with eccentricity e = 10 mm. Find the maximum and minimum longitudinal normal stresses, ignoring buckling.

Hint

Would a cut through the section carry only an axial force?

Show worked solution

Direct compression is P/A = 60,000/2,000 = 30 MPa. Eccentricity adds M = Pe = 600,000 N·mm, so bending stress is M/W = 15 MPa.

σmax,comp = 30 + 15 = 45 MPa compression
σopposite edge = 30 − 15 = 15 MPa compression

Even though the load is compressive, the section stress is not uniform. For a larger eccentricity, one edge can enter tension.

P5

Choosing the applicable model

hard

A pinned steel column has E = 200 GPa, yield stress 250 MPa, and radius of gyration r = 25 mm. Estimate the slenderness ratio where Euler stress equals yield stress. What does the comparison tell you?

Hint

Express the critical load as a stress and compare material limits.

Show worked solution

Euler stress is σE = Pcr/A = π²E/(KL/r)². Set it equal to yield stress and K = 1:

(KL/r)transition = π√(E/σy) = π√(200,000/250) ≈ 88.9

Above this slenderness, the ideal Euler stress is below yield, so elastic buckling is plausible. Below it, yielding may occur first and Euler alone overstates the capacity. This is a boundary estimate, not a code design rule.

P6

A slightly crooked member

challenge

A column has first-mode initial crookedness amplitude e₀ and carries P = 0.6Pcr. Choose the appropriate one-mode elastic relation from the lesson and find the total-bow amplification factor. What happens as P approaches Pcr?

Hint

Distinguish total bow from additional load-induced displacement.

Show worked solution
e/e₀ = 1/(1 − 0.6) = 2.5

The crookedness is amplified to 2.5 times its initial amplitude in this simplified model. As P approaches Pcr from below, the denominator approaches zero and the linear model predicts rapidly growing deflection. Real columns yield, develop nonlinear geometry, and fail before an actual infinite displacement.

Think of the analysis in order: check axial deformation; identify the buckling axis and effective length; estimate Euler load if the member is slender and elastic; then account for eccentricity, imperfections, yielding, and bracing before calling it safe.
