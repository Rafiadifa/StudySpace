Problem 1 · Simply supported beam under full-span uniform load

A simply supported beam of span (l) carries a uniform load (q) over its full length. Find the bending moment diagram and normal stresses at (C_1,C_2,C_3) on the midspan section.

1. Reactions and internal forces

The loading and support arrangement are symmetric, so vertical equilibrium gives equal reactions:

RA = RB = ql/2

Take (x) from A. For a cut at (x), the left-side free body gives:

V(x) = ql/2 − qx
M(x) = (ql/2)x − qx²/2 = qx(l − x)/2,   0 ≤ x ≤ l

The shear crosses zero at (x=l/2), so the bending moment is maximum at C:

MC = Mmax = ql²/8

2. Cross-section and point coordinates

For the rectangle, (I_z=bh^3/12). The assignment’s section sketch takes (y) positive downward. Therefore (C_1) is at (y=-h/2), (C_2) is on the neutral axis at (y=0), and (C_3) is at (y=+h/4).

3. Normal stresses at C

Use the course flexure equation (sigma=My/I_z), with tensile stress positive. Substituting (M_C=ql^2/8) and (I_z=bh^3/12):

σC₁ = (ql²/8)(−h/2)/(bh³/12) = −3ql²/(4bh²)   compression
σC₂ = (ql²/8)(0)/(bh³/12) = 0
σC₃ = (ql²/8)(h/4)/(bh³/12) = +3ql²/(8bh²)   tension
Answer: (C_1) is in compression, (C_2) has zero bending stress because it lies on the neutral axis, and (C_3) is in tension. The stress magnitude at (C_1) is twice that at (C_3).

Problem 2 · Cantilever timber beam with an end load

Given (l=2 ext{ m}), (F=10 ext{ kN}), circular diameter (d=300 ext{ mm}), and allowable bending stress ([sigma]=10 ext{ MPa}).

1. Bending moment diagram

The maximum moment occurs at the fixed end. With (x) measured from the wall, its magnitude is (F(l-x)). The bending is hogging, so the top side is in tension; draw the moment diagram on that side.

|Mmax| = Fl = (10 kN)(2 m) = 20 kN·m

2. Circular section modulus and maximum stress

W = I/c = πd³/32 = π(300 mm)³/32 = 2.651 × 10⁶ mm³
|Mmax| = 20 kN·m = 20 × 10⁶ N·mm
σmax = |Mmax|/W = (20 × 10⁶)/(2.651 × 10⁶) = 7.55 MPa
Strength check: (7.55 ext{ MPa}<10 ext{ MPa}), so the timber beam satisfies the bending normal-stress criterion. The calculated stress is about (75.5\%) of the allowable stress.

Problem 3 · Design a rectangular section

Given a simply supported beam with (l=2 ext{ m}), (q=15 ext{ kN/m}), (h:b=2:1), and ([sigma]=160 ext{ MPa}).

1. Reactions, shear, and bending moment

RA = RB = ql/2 = (15)(2)/2 = 15 kN

For (x) measured from A in metres:

V(x) = 15 − 15x   kN
M(x) = 15x − 7.5x²   kN·m,  0 ≤ x ≤ 2

Since (dM/dx=V), the moment reaches an extreme where (V=0). Thus the maximum occurs at midspan:

Mmax = M(1) = 7.5 kN·m = 7.5 × 10⁶ N·mm

2. Required section modulus and dimensions

For a rectangle with vertical depth (h), (W_z=bh^2/6). The strength condition requires:

Wrequired = Mmax/[σ] = (7.5 × 10⁶ N·mm)/(160 N/mm²) = 46,875 mm³

Substitute (h=2b):

Wz = b(2b)²/6 = 2b³/3 ≥ 46,875
b³ ≥ 70,312.5 mm³
bmin = 41.27 mm,   hmin = 82.55 mm

These are the theoretical minimum dimensions. Round upward for a practical choice; for example, (b=45 ext{ mm}), (h=90 ext{ mm}) preserves the required ratio.

Wz = (45)(90²)/6 = 60,750 mm³
σmax = (7.5 × 10⁶)/60,750 = 123.46 MPa < 160 MPa
Answer: the theoretical minimum is approximately (41.27 ext{ mm} imes 82.55 ext{ mm}) (width × depth). A reasonable rounded-up design is 45 mm × 90 mm, which satisfies the bending-strength criterion.

Sign convention: sagging moment is positive and produces bottom tension/top compression. The moment diagrams above are placed on the tension side; the signed stresses in Problem 1 use the downward-positive (y)-axis shown in the assignment section sketch.
