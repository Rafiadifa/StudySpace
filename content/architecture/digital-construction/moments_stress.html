      Part A · Statics 02 review

      Moments and equilibrium

      Week 3 (静力学02) gave you three tools: the moment of a force, the couple, and the three equilibrium equations. Every HW2 question uses one or more of them. Read the five ideas, then work the problems.

      1

      A moment is force × perpendicular distance

      The moment of a force about a point O (力矩) measures how strongly the force turns the body around O. Physics calls the same thing torque.

      M_O = ± F · d unit: kN·m

      d is the shortest distance from O to the force's line of action, the force extended in both directions. It is not the distance from O to the point where the force is applied. In the sketch below, r is that point-to-point distance and is the wrong one to use.

      Direction: pretend the body is pinned at O, push at the point in the direction of the force, and see which way it swings. Choose counterclockwise as positive, write that down, and keep it for the whole problem.

      2

      Tilted forces: use trig once

      There are two equivalent ways. Shrink the distance (d = r·sinθ) and keep F whole, or shrink the force (F⊥ = F·sinθ) and keep r whole. Never do both.

      The third way is often easiest: split F into Fx and Fy and add their moments. This is the resultant-moment theorem (合力矩定理). Each component has a simple horizontal or vertical lever arm.

      Quick test for sin or cos: let the angle go to 0. If the force then points straight along the line to O, the moment must become 0.

      3

      A couple turns but never pushes

      A couple (力偶) is two equal, opposite, parallel forces a distance d apart. Their net force is zero, so a couple adds nothing to ΣX or ΣY. Its turning effect M = F·d is the same about every point, so in a moment equation you just add ±M, with no lever arm.

      4

      Equilibrium: three equations, three unknowns

      ΣX = 0 ΣY = 0 ΣM_O = 0 (any point O)

      Follow the slides' three steps: isolate the body (隔离体), draw every force on it (受力图), and write the equations. Take moments about a point where unknown forces cross, so each equation has only one unknown.

      SupportUnknowns it adds
      Roller 辊轴支座1, perpendicular to the surface
      Pin 固定铰支座2: X and Y
      Fixed end 固定端3: X, Y and a moment
      5

      Two-force bars

      A bar pinned at both ends with no load in between is a two-force bar (二力杆). Its force acts along the line joining the two pins, with the same size at both ends. That one shared force is how a linkage passes load from one part to the next.

      Slips to watch for (they came up in HW2)

      • Writing a moment in kN. A moment is always force × length: kN·m.
      • Multiplying by the distance between two points when the force is tilted. Use the perpendicular distance.
      • Applying sinθ to both the force and the distance.
      • Putting a couple into ΣY. It only appears in ΣM.
      • In a ratio, using a different unknown force on each side. Find the one force they share.

      Axial tension and compression

      Statics treated every body as rigid. Mechanics of materials (材料力学) asks what happens inside a member: how hard the material is working, and how much it stretches. The first case is a straight bar loaded along its axis. This preview follows Part 2, Chapter 1 of your course slides.

      1

      Internal force and the method of sections

      To find the force inside a bar, cut it (截面法). Keep one piece, draw the internal force N on the cut face, and use ΣX = 0. N is the axial force (轴力).

      Sign: tension is positive, compression is negative. Always draw the unknown N as tension, pointing away from the piece. If the answer comes out negative, the segment is in compression, and you don't need to redraw anything.

      2

      Axial force diagram

      An axial force diagram (轴力图) plots N along the bar: positive above the baseline, negative below. N is constant between loads and jumps by the size of each load applied along the bar.

      3

      Stress: force per area

      Two bars of the same material carry the same N. The thinner one fails first, so the internal force alone doesn't tell you how hard the material is working. Stress does. For an axial bar, the normal stress (正应力) is spread evenly over the cross-section:

      σ = N / A

      The sign of σ follows N. Units: 1 Pa = 1 N/m², and 1 MPa = 1 N/mm². The quickest route is to convert kN to N (× 1000), keep the area in mm², and the answer comes out directly in MPa.

      4

      Strain and Hooke's law

      Strain is the change in length divided by the original length. It has no unit. In the elastic range, stress is proportional to strain (虎克定律). E is the elastic modulus (弹性模量), a property of the material.

      ε = Δl / l σ = E · ε → Δl = N · l / (E · A) (EA = axial stiffness 抗拉刚度)
      MaterialE, roughlyin N/mm²
      Steel 钢200 GPa200 000
      Aluminium 铝70 GPa70 000
      Concrete 混凝土30 GPa30 000
      Timber, along grain 木10 GPa10 000

      A stretched bar also gets thinner. The sideways strain is ε₁ = −μ·ε, where μ is Poisson's ratio (泊松比), about 0.3 for steel.

      5

      What a tensile test shows

      Pull a mild steel specimen until it breaks and plot σ against ε. The curve has four stages.

      Elastic: a straight line up to the proportional limit σp, with slope E. Yield: the strain grows while the stress stays near the yield limit σs. Strain hardening: the curve rises to the strength limit σb. Necking: one spot thins until it breaks.

      Plastic materials like steel are useless once they yield, so their ultimate stress is σ0 = σs. Brittle materials like concrete and cast iron break without yielding, so σ0 = σb.

      6

      Strength condition

      Designers never work up to σ0. They divide by a safety factor n > 1 to get the allowable stress (容许应力), then require the largest stress to stay below it:

      [σ] = σ₀ / n σ_max = N_max / A ≤ [σ]

      The same inequality does three jobs:

      JobYou knowYou find
      Check 强度校核N, A, [σ]Is N/A ≤ [σ]?
      Size 选择截面N, [σ]A ≥ N / [σ]
      Allowable load 容许荷载A, [σ]N ≤ [σ]·A, then the load
      7

      When statics runs out

      Sometimes there are more unknown forces than equilibrium equations, as when a bar touches a second support or a beam hangs on three rods. Such a structure is statically indeterminate. The missing equation comes from geometry: the pieces must still fit together after they deform. This is deformation compatibility (变形协调). Write it in terms of Δl, then replace each Δl with N·l/(EA). The last two problems use this.
